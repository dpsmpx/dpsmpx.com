I'm dpsmpx, a native systems developer: between 11 September and 5 October 2026, one game grew into an ecosystem of 9 repositories.

The ecosystem

The repositories are independent: no repository depends on another to build or run. Decisions, contracts and facts tie them together.

  • Products. Galdhold — a voxel action-RPG with an open procedural world (project page). DPlang — early stage: the compiler is in development and builds programs for Linux, Windows and Android (project page).
  • Engineering foundation. DP-Verify — a shared engineering-checks framework for the author's repositories.
  • Knowledge and publishing. SocialMediaImage — the content system behind dpsmpx.com; DPprofile — a verifiable history of the development path — sources, facts, timeline; DPlearn — a learning system where “mastered” is computed from evidence; DP_game_market — reproducible game market analysis.
  • Meta layer. DPmeta — the ecosystem map, contracts between repositories and risks.

One more project has no public name. The core main branches hold 344k lines of code, tests and docs as of 5 October 2026.

Galdhold

Galdhold (formerly VoxelRPG): C++20 and Vulkan, my own engine, kept separate from the game. It has been tested on Android phones (Redmi Note 13, Poco X7 Pro 5G). The project has over 150,000 lines of C++ (October 2026) and over 5,000 automated checks.

How I work

AI agents write most of the code; architecture and final decisions stay with me. The AI share shows in the history: 981 commits in 24 days, 83% of them by AI sessions. In short, the AI can forget the session; the project does not have to: the decision logs hold 210 entries as of 5 October 2026.
